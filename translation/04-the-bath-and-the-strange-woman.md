## 17. The warning against the bath [AC. i.; cf. AC i.2]

**Ge'ez (transcription, largely reconstructed; see note above):**

> ወኣመ ⌈ዘትታጥብ⌉ ⌈ምስለ⌉ ⌈ንስቲት⌉ ⌈ኢትእቲ⌉ ⌈ውስተ⌉ ⌈ማእበል⌉። ከመ ⌈ኢይርኡ⌉ ⌈ጥበበ⌉ ⌈ዕብየት⌉ከ ወ⌈ኳእምረ⌉ ⌈ሥጋ⌉ከ ወ⌈ኢትኩነ⌉
> ⌈ስንእስታ⌉ እ⌈ሙንቲ⌉ሆ⌈ን⌉ ወ⌈ንተ⌉ ⌈እሙንት⌉ ⌈ትኩነ⌉ እ⌈ሙንተ⌉።

**Translation:**

> And when thou desirest to bathe, enter not thou into the bath where the women bathe; lest
> they see the beauty of thy youth and the comeliness of thy flesh, and lest thou become a
> stumbling-block unto them, and thou thyself sin and fall into error with them.

**Notes:**
1. P: "Thou moreover, when thou wilt bathe thyself, enter not into the bath where the women
   wash themselves, lest they see the beauty of thy youth and the fairness of thy flesh, and
   thou be a stumbling-block to them, and thyself also shouldst sin and fall into error with
   them." H: "Moreover, when thou desirest to bathe, enter not into the bath where the women
   bathe, lest they see the beauty of thy youth, and the charm of thy flesh, and stumble
   because of thee, or thou thyself sin and fall by reason of them."
2. AC i.2 (Greek) has the same rule in shorter form: "When thou goest to the bath, go not with
   the women"; the "beauty of thy youth" clause is the Ethiopian/Syriac expansion.
3. The Ge'ez of this verse is not recoverable from the 1834 print as OCR'd; the transcription
   above is a reconstruction from the cognate passages (the bath clause of the Didascalia is
   formulaic). It is retained here as a placeholder, not as evidence.

## 18. [i., 7.] "Say unto Wisdom, Thou art my sister" — Prov 7:1–9

**Ge'ez (transcription, partly restored):**

> ⌈ለብው⌉ ⌈ወናዕምሩ⌉ ⌈ፍትወተ⌉ ⌈ቅዱ⌉⌈ስ⌉ ⌈ዘ⌈ይቤ⌉። ⌈ጠ⌉⌈በቡ⌉ ⌈ትእዛዤ⌉ ⋯ ይ⌈ቤ⌉ ⌈ለ⌉ጥ⌈በ⌉ብ ∅⌈ን⌉≱ ⌈ሕ⌈ተ⌉⌈ይ⌉ ወ⌈ለ⌉⌈ኣ⌉⌈ም⌉∭
> ⌈ገ∓⌉∭⌈ት⌉ከ ⌈ር⌉∉⌈ይ⌉ ከመ ⌈ትዕ⌉ቀ⌈ብ⌉ከ ⌈ወ⌉⌈አ⌉⌈ማ⌉∓⌈ብ⌉∭ከ ⌈አ⌉⌈ን⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ⌈ነ∓⌉∥⌈ር⌉ ወ⌈ዘ⌉ማ⌈ው⌉ያ⌈ን⌉። ኣ⌈ስ⌉መ ⌈ኣ⌉⌈መ⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈ኮ⌉ተ
> ⌈ቤ⌉⌈ታ⌉ ው⌈ስ⌉ተ ⌈መ⌉ራ⌈ሕ⌉⌈ብ⌉ት ። ⋯ ወኢ⌈ትወስክ⌉ ⋯ ⌈ሎ⌉≠ ⋯ እ⌈ሎ⌉ ∏⌈ፎ⌉≠ ጥ⌈በበ⌉ ⌈ር⌉∉⌈ይ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ∏⌈ሎ⌉⌈ቱ⌉ እን⌈ዘ⌉ ት⌈ን⌉⌈ው⌉ም
> ⌈ውስተ⌉ ጽ⌈ልመ⌉ተ ⌈ማ⌉⌈ዕ⌉⌈ል⌉ተ ወ⌈ሌ⌉⌈ሊ⌉ተ።

**Translation:**

> My children, let us hear and understand the word of the Holy One, which saith: ⌈Keep ye my
> commandment⌉; say unto Wisdom, Thou art my sister, and make understanding thy friend; that
> she may keep thee, ⌈and thou sin not⌉, from the strange and evil woman, even if she bring
> unto thee words of allurement. For from the window of her house she looketh down into the
> streets; ⌈and if she see one of the foolish⌉ ⌈children⌉, a youth void of understanding, ⌈who
> walketh⌉ ⌈in the corners of the ways of her house⌉, talking ⌈in the darkness⌉ of evening and
> of night.

**Notes:**
1. H: "O my children, let us hear and understand the word of the Holy One, which saith, Keep ye
   my commandment; say unto wisdom, Thou art my sister, and make knowledge thy friend, that she
   may keep thee from the strange and evil woman, even if she bring unto thee words of
   allurement. For from the window of her house she looketh down on the streets, and if she see
   one of the foolish children, a youth that lacketh knowledge, who walketh in the corners of
   the ways of her house, and talketh in the darkness of evening, when the quiet and darkness of
   night come on." P is the same, reading "the Holy Word" and "Knowledge thy familiar friend …
   though she bring unto thee enticing words … the quietness and darkness of night is come on."
2. Prov 7:1–4 with v. 5 blended into the participles ("the strange and evil woman" =
   ነኪር ወዘማውያን, lit. "the strange and the adulteress"); Prov 7:6–9 in the rest ("window of her
   house," "streets," "youth void of understanding," "corners of the ways," "darkness of evening
   and night"). The Ethiopic Didascalia, like the Syriac, quotes Proverbs from its own Bible
   text, not the Septuagint order.
3. "words of allurement": restored from ⌈ወኣመ ⋯ ዘርቅ ⋯ ΔῊ(ንስቲት?) ⋯ AN⌉ (conj.); P "enticing
   words", H "words of allurement." Syriac: "though she bring words to seduce thee."
4. ጥበብ = Wisdom/Sophia ( Ethiopic ጥበብ, lit. "wisdom"); "understanding" = ⌈ኣምር⌉ restored.

## 19. [i., 7.] The ornaments of adultery — Prov 7:10–14

**Ge'ez (transcription, partly restored):**

> ወ⌈ዘገባ⌉። ብ⌈ሓ⌉ሲት ⌈ላዕለ⌉ ሎቱ ⌈ሰብእ⌉ ነገሠት በ⌈ጥ⌉⌈ብ⌉ን እ⌈ሎ⌉ ⌈ባቲ⌉ ⌈ት⌉⌈ር⌉ሲ⌈ተ⌉ ⋯ ኣ⌈ም⌉∥ ⋯ ወ⌈ው⌉⌈ር⌉⌈ዙ⌉ተ።
> ⌈ሠ⌉⌈ር⌉ርት ⌈ወ⌉ ገ⌈ብ⌉⌈ር⌉ት ወ⌈በ⌉ቤ⌈ታ⌉ኒ ኢ⌈ያ⌉⌈ዕ⌉⌈ር⌉⌈ፈ⌉ ⌈ዓ⌉⌈ገ⌉⌈ሪ⌉ዛ⌈ሣ⌉ ወ⌈መ⌉⌈ዋ⌉⌈ዕ⌉⌈ላ⌉ለ ⋯ ው⌈ስ⌉ተ ⌈መ⌉ራ⌈ሕ⌉⌈ሐ⌉⌈ብ⌉ት ወ⌈ው⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈ተ⌉
> ⌈መ⌉⌈ዓ⌉⌈ጓ⌉⌈ን⌉ን። ወ⌈አ⌉⌈ን⌉⌈ተ⌉⌈ስ⌉ተ እ⌈ሎ⌉ የ⌈ገብ⌉∩ ∏⌈እ⌉⌈ሲ⌉⌈ት⌉ ዛ⌈ቲ⌉ ⌈ት⌉⌈ቤ⌉ በ⌈ገ⌉⌈ጽ⌉ ⊠⌈መ⌉⌈ሥ⌉⌈ዋ⌉⌈ዕ⌉ተ ⌈በ⌉⌈ላ⌉⌈ም⌉ ው⌈ኣ⌉⌈ቱ⌉
> ሆ⌈ም⌉ ው⌈እ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ≠⌈ብ⌉≠⌈ዕ⌉⌈ት⌉⌈ሮ⌉።

**Translation:**

> And, behold, there met him a woman, in the adornment(?) of a harlot, ⌈and subtle of heart⌉;
> and the adornments wherewith the hearts of the youths are stirred. She is ⌈clamorous⌉ and
> ⌈wanton⌉, and her feet abide not in her house; ⌈now⌉ she is ⌈abroad⌉, and now in the streets,
> and ⌈lieth in wait⌉ at every corner. And she caught him, that man, and kissed him, and with a
> face ⌈without shame⌉ said unto him: ⌈I have⌉ a peace-offering; to-day ⌈I have paid⌉ my vows.

**Notes:**
1. H: "then the woman lighteth upon him having the adornments of adultery, which excite the
   hearts of the youths. She is flighty and wanton, and in her house her feet rest not; and for
   a short while she wandereth abroad, and for a while in the streets, and in every corner she
   lieth in wait. And she laid hold on him and kissed him, and with a face without shame said
   unto him, I have a peace-offering; to-day I pay my vow." P is close ("She is rambling and
   wanton … the ornaments of adultery, which make the hearts of young men flutter").
2. Prov 7:10–14 following the Didascalia's own text; "the adornments wherewith the hearts of
   the youths are stirred" expands Prov 7:10b in the Didascalian manner (Syriac: "the garments
   of the harlot's adornment, which change the mind of the young men").
3. "peace-offering" = ⌈መሥዋዕተ ሰላም⌉ (Lev 7:11ff.; the "በገጽ መሥዋዕተ በላም" of the print = በገጽ ክፉእ(?)
   "with a shameless face" + መሥዋዕተ ሰላም run together by the OCR).

## 20. [i., 7.] The seduction and the warning — Prov 7:15–27 (continued citations)

**Ge'ez (transcription, partly restored):**

> ⌈ው⌉⌈ስ⌉ተ ማ⌈ዕ⌉⌈ል⌉⌈ተ⌉ ⌈ዛቲ⌉ ረ⌈ከ⌉⌈ብ⌉ኩ። ⌈በ⌉⌈ቀ⌉ ⌈ካ⌉⌈ዌ⌉⌈ይ⌉ ገ⌈ለ⌉⌈ዥ⌉⌈ኩ⌉ ም⌈ስ⌉⌈ከ⌉⌈ፎ⌉⌈ን⌉ ዘ⌈ር⌉⌈ጻ⌉⌈ፍ⌉⌈ኩ⌉ ዘ⌈ኣ⌉⌈ም⌉⌈ግ⌉⌈ብ⌉⌈ጽ⌉።
> ⌈ድ⌉⌈ቤ⌉ አ⌈ት⌉⌈ጌ⌉⌈ጽ⌉ር ⋯ ወ⌈ቤ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ይ⌈ው⌉⌈ዐ⌉ ⋯ ⌈ቤ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ኢ⌈ዮ⌉⌈ን⌉⌈በ⌉ ⌈ኃ⌉⌈ወ⌉⌈ለ⌉ ≀⌈ት⌉⌈ዓ⌉በ ⋯ ⋈⌈ታ⌉ው⌈ስ⌉⌈ደ⌉ ⌈በግ⌉⌈ዕ⌉⌈ዝ⌉
> ⋯ ⋈⌈ኢ⌉⌈ይ⌉⌈መጽ⌉⌈እ⌉ ⋯ ወ⌈ዘ⌉⌈ከመ⌉⌈ሆ⌉ ኣ⌈ም⌉⌈ሐ⌉⌈ሞ⌉⌈ት⌉ ⌈ት⌉⌈ረ⌉⌈ክ⌉⌈ብ⌉ ወ⌈ስ⌉⌈ሑ⌉⌈ል⌉ ፈ⌈ድ⌉⌈ፈ⌉⌈ጀ⌉ ወ⌈ጥ⌉⌈ባ⌉⌈ሣ⌉⌈ት⌉ ዘ⌈ክ⌉⌈ል⌉⌈ኤ⌉።

**Translation:**

> ⌈In the day⌉ I ⌈sought⌉ ⌈my peace-offerings⌉; ⌈to-day⌉ I ⌈have paid⌉ my vows. ⌈Therefore⌉ came I
> forth to meet thee, ⌈desiring⌉ thy countenance, and I have found thee. I have ⌈spread⌉ my bed
> with ⌈fine linen⌉, ⌈carpets from Egypt⌉ I have spread out; I have ⌈sprinkled⌉ my bed with
> ⌈saffron⌉, and my house with ⌈cinnamon⌉. ⌈Come⌉, let us enjoy ourselves with kisses till the
> morning, and ⌈wanton in love⌉: for my husband is not in the house; he is gone on a far journey,
> taking ⌈his bag of gold⌉ in his hand, ⌈for many days⌉ ere he return into the house. Thus she
> deceived him with many words, and drew him in with the snare of her lips; and he followed her
> in the evil desire of his soul, and in the blindness of his heart. And again it is said: Look
> not on an evil woman; for honey floweth from the lips of an adulterous woman, which for a
> time is sweet to thy throat, and afterwards thou shalt find it ⌈more bitter⌉ than ⌈gall⌉, and
> ⌈sharper⌉ ⌈than a two-edged sword⌉.

**Notes:**
1. H (p. 9): "therefore I came to meet thee; desiring thy countenance I have found thee. I have
   spread my bed with fine linen, carpets from Egypt I have strewn; I have sprinkled my bed with
   saffron, and my house with cinnamon. Come, let us enjoy ourselves with kisses until the
   morning, and let us revel in love. For my husband is not in the house; he is gone … [snare
   of her lips], and he followed her in the wicked blindness of his heart. And furthermore it is
   said, Look not on an evil woman, for honey floweth from the lips of an adulterous woman,
   which for a time is sweet to thy throat, and afterwards thou shalt find it more bitter than
   gall, and sharper by far than a two-edged sword."
2. Prov 7:15–21 for the seduction; Prov 5:3–4 for the honey-and-gall citation (Harden's
   reference to Pr 5, 3f.). Ge'ez anchors: ⌈ዘከመሆ ኣምሐሞት ትረክብ ወስሑል ፈድፈጀ ወጥባሣት ዘክልኤ⌉ ("thou
   shalt find [it] … bitter …, and two-edged"); the "bag of gold" clause (Ge'ez ⌈ጥቁሪ ወርቅ⌉
   restored, "a bag of gold") is a Didascalian expansion of Prov 7:20 ("he hath taken a bag of
   money with him").
3. The clause "he is gone on a far journey, taking his bag of gold in his hand, for many days
   ere he return into the house" closes P's page and opens the next print-block with the
   seducer's speech (Ge'ez ⌈ውስተ ቤቱ ጥረ ርሑቅ ⋯ ጥቁሪ ወርቅ ውስተ እዴሆ ⋯ በመዋዕል ብዙእ ⊢ገብአ ውስተ ቤቱ⌉).

## 21. [i., 7.] "Keep thy way far from her" — Prov 5:7–8 and the repentance of the fool

**Ge'ez (transcription, partly restored):**

> ወ⌈ካዕ⌉⌈በ⌉ ⌈ድ⌉⌈ቤ⌉ ⌈ር⌉⌈ሑ⌉⌈ቀ⌉ ⍈⌈ኖ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ⊥⌈ሎ⌉ በ⌈ው⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ⌈ቤ⌉⌈ታ⌉ ከ⌈መ⌉ ⌈ኢት⌉⌈ወ⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈ክ⌉ ⌈ለ⌉⌈ባ⌉⌈ዕ⌉⌈ድ⌉ ⌈ሕ⌉⌈ይ⌉⌈ወ⌉⌈ተ⌉⌈ከ⌉
> ወ⌈ን⌉⌈ብ⌉⌈ዖ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ⊥⌈እ⌉⌈ሎ⌉ ዘ⌈እ⌉⌈ን⌉⌈በ⌉⌈ለ⌉ ⌈መ⌉⌈ሕ⌉⌈ረ⌉⌈ት⌉። ከ⌈መ⌉ ⌈ኢ⌉⌈ይ⌉⌈ጽ⌉⌈ገ⌉⌈ቡ⌉ ⌈ን⌉⌈ኪ⌉⌈ሪ⌉⌈ን⌉ ገ⌈ደ⌉⌈ማ⌉⌈ን⌉ ዘ⌈ት⌉⌈ጌ⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈ሕ⌉
> ⋍⌈ስ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ⌈ቤ⌉⌈ተ⌉ ⌈ባ⌉⌈ዕ⌉⌈ድ⌉ ⊠⌈ኣ⌉⌈መ⌉ ⊰⌈ደ⌉⌈ክ⌉⌈መ⌉ ⊰⌈ሥ⌉⌈ጋ⌉⌈ከ⌉ ⌈ነ⌉⌈ፍ⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈ት⌉። ወ⌈ተ⌉⌈ስ⌉⌈እ⌉⌈ል⌉ ⌈እ⌉⌈ፍ⌉ ⌈ጸ⌉⌈ላ⌉⌈እ⌉⌈ኩ⌉
> ⌈ት⌉⌈ም⌉⌈ህ⌉⌈ር⌉⌈ተ⌉ ወ⌈አ⌉⌈መ⌉⌈ተ⌉⌈ግ⌉⌈ሣ⌉⌈ጽ⌉ ⌈ተ⌉⌈ግ⌉⌈ሕ⌉⌈ሀ⌉⌈ሠ⌉ ⌈ኣ⌉⌈ዝ⌉⌈ን⌉⌈ሮ⌉ ወ⌈አ⌉⋯

**Translation:**

> And again: Keep thy way far from her, and draw not near to the door of her house; lest thou
> give thy life to strangers, and thy labours to them that are without mercy; lest strangers be
> filled with thy strength, and thy labour enter into the house of a stranger, ⌈when the flesh
> of thy body⌉ ⌈faileth⌉. And thou shalt repent at the last ⌈and say⌉: How have I hated
> instruction, and admonition ⌈hath my soul refused⌉…

**Notes:**
1. H: "And again it is said, Keep thy way from her, and draw not nigh to the door of her house,
   lest thou give thy life to others, and thy livelihood to them that are without mercy; lest
   strangers be filled with thy strength, and thy labour pass to the house of another; and thou
   shalt repent afterwards when thy flesh (and) thy body are weakened, and thou shalt say, How
   have I hated instruction …" P agrees ("Turn thy way far from her … when the flesh of thy body
   faileth").
2. Prov 5:8 (Harden: Pr 5, 8) with the Didascalian expansion; "at the last" (Ge'ez ⌈ኣመ ተገብርክ(?)
   ⋯⌉ uncertain) renders H "afterwards", P "afterwards."

## 22. [i., 7.] Close: "How have I hated instruction"; choose the good things [end of Section I]

**Translation:**

> [I have not hearkened to] the voice of them that gave me commandments, who admonished me ⌈and
> taught me⌉, nor caused mine ear to attend: I have been well nigh in all evil. And now I say
> unto you, ⌈Choose out⌉ all the ⌈good⌉ ⌈things which are written⌉ in the Holy Scriptures, that
> we neglect them not, nor turn aside unto a multitude of words, but may be ⌈pure⌉ in ⌈the right
> faith⌉ and in ⌈good-doing⌉, and depart far from all evil before God the Lord, that we may find
> life eternal.

**Notes:**
1. H: "and I have not hearkened to the voice of them that gave me commandments, who admonished
   me and taught me, nor caused mine ear to attend: I have been well nigh in all evil. And now
   also I say unto you, Choose out all the good things which are written in the Holy Scriptures,
   that we may not neglect these witnesses, and turn aside unto a multitude of words, but may be
   strong in faith, and in doing good. And let us depart far from all evil-doing, and be pure
   from all evil (and) uncleanness before God, that we may find life eternal." P: "rebuked me
   and instructed me, and have not caused my ear to listen: almost have I been in all evil. And
   now I say unto you, choose for yourselves all good things, which are written in the Holy
   Scriptures, that we may not neglect this testimony, and turn away into a multitude of words,
   but may abide in the right faith, and in doing that which is good. And let us depart from
   every evil work, and be clean from all impurity before the Lord, that we may obtain
   everlasting life."
2. "these witnesses" (H) vs "this testimony" (P): Ge'ez ⌈ጽራጁ⌉ restored from ⌈ጽራጁ⌉/መስክር family
   (witness/testimony); singular in P, plural in H — the print does not decide.
3. This closes the first great admonition (Harden's [i., 7]); P's Chapter II "That it is the
   duty of Women to obey their Husbands, and to walk in wisdom and virtue" (Harden's [i., 8],
   with 1 Cor 11:3 and Prov 31) is the next file, `05-wives-and-husbands.md`.
4. Not in AC; the whole Proverbs vii instruction belongs to the material that Additional Note A
   traces in the Syriac and Arabic recensions (Harden, p. 189 sq.).
